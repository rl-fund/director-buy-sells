# Chemist Warehouse (ASX:SIG) Deep Research Report

**Date:** February 2026
**Prepared for:** Hedge Fund Quality Analysis
**Focus:** Three Key Investment Questions

---

## Executive Summary

Chemist Warehouse (CW), now trading as part of Sigma Healthcare (ASX:SIG) following the $32B merger in February 2025, is Australia's largest discount pharmacy chain by revenue. The merged entity operates 881 franchise pharmacies domestically across four brands (Chemist Warehouse, My Chemist, Amcal, Discount Drug Stores) plus 70+ international stores. CW generates ~$10.3B in annual retail network sales with 11%+ LFL growth and operates on a unique franchise model that circumvents Australian pharmacy ownership regulations.

**Key Findings:**
- **Domestic Store Growth:** 25-33 new stores per year sustainable for next 5-10 years; runway to ~830 stores by matching Victoria density nationally
- **LFL Growth Sustainability:** 10%+ growth achievable for 2-3 years driven by GLP-1s, demographics, and product mix; will moderate to 5-7% thereafter
- **International Potential:** 100+ stores in NZ, UK entry imminent, Ireland scaling to 20+, UAE early stage; UK is the major prize

## SECTION 1: DOMESTIC STORE GROWTH ANALYSIS

### 1.1 Current State

| Metric | Value (June 2025) |
|--------|-------------------|
| CW/My Chemist Stores (Australia) | 588 |
| Total Group Stores (incl. Amcal, DDS) | 881 |
| Historical Growth Rate | 30-35 stores/year |
| FY25 New Store Openings | 35 (domestic + international) |

### 1.2 Can CW Continue Growing Domestic Stores?

**YES - But the mechanics reveal the "why":**

#### 1.2.1 The Victoria Density Thesis

**This is the single most important insight for domestic growth:**

| State | CW Stores | Population per CW Store | Share of CW Network |
|-------|-----------|-------------------------|---------------------|
| Victoria | ~235 | 30,560 | ~40% |
| NSW | ~135 | 64,354 | ~23% |
| Queensland | ~105 | 51,436 | ~18% |
| Other | ~110 | Various | ~19% |

**Key Finding:** Victoria has roughly **2x the store density** of NSW and Queensland on a population-adjusted basis.

**WHY does Victoria have higher density?**
- CW was founded in Melbourne (1972/2000) - home market advantage
- Earlier market entry = more sites locked up
- Deeper franchisee relationships in Victoria
- Brand recognition stronger in home market

**WHY can this density be replicated elsewhere?**
- Same regulatory framework (state pharmacy laws similar)
- CW brand is now national - advertising spend ($100M/year) creates demand everywhere
- Demonstrated model portability (works in NZ, Ireland)
- Population growth concentrated in NSW/QLD suburbs - new retail developments

**Growth Math:**
- If CW achieved Victorian density nationally: **~830 stores** (vs. 588 today)
- That's **242 additional stores** or ~10 years at 25 stores/year
- Analyst estimates: Citi forecasts 25 net new stores/year in Australia through FY30

### 1.2.2 Where Do New Stores Come From?

**Source 1: Pipeline Stores (Acquisitions)**
- CW maintains a "pipeline" of stores acquired by pharmacists with intention of converting to CW
- As of June 2024: **23 Pipeline Stores** waiting for conversion
- These are typically independent pharmacies purchased, then rebranded/reformatted

**WHY can CW acquire independents?**
1. **Retiring pharmacists:** ~31% of Australia's 5,900 pharmacies are in regional areas, often owned by aging pharmacists (average age ~55+)
2. **Financial pressure:** 60-day dispensing reform reduces dispensary revenue by ~$170K/year per pharmacy
3. **CW offers exit:** Sell to a CW-backed pharmacist, get liquidity, pharmacy continues operating
4. **Relative attractiveness:** Independent pharmacy profit margins squeezed (competing with CW on price is impossible)

**Source 2: Greenfield Sites (New Retail Developments)**
- Australia's population grows ~1.5%/year, concentrated in Sydney/Melbourne/Brisbane suburbs
- New shopping centres, retail parks, medical centres all need pharmacy anchors
- CW is often preferred tenant (high foot traffic, destination retailer)

**Source 3: Competitive Conversions**
- Sigma merger brought Amcal (~200 stores) and Discount Drug Stores (~140 stores) into the group
- Opportunity to convert underperforming banners to CW format over time
- However, management has indicated they will maintain multi-brand strategy

### 1.2.3 Constraints on Domestic Growth

**Regulatory Constraints:**

1. **Pharmacy Location Rules (Federal)**
   - New pharmacies cannot open within 1.5km-10km of existing pharmacy (varies by location)
   - Relocation limited to 1km every 5 years
   - Large medical centres (8+ doctors) can get new pharmacy approval

2. **Ownership Regulations (State)**
   - Pharmacies must be majority owned by registered pharmacists
   - Limits on number of pharmacies per owner: 4-6 depending on state

   | State | Max Pharmacies per Owner |
   |-------|--------------------------|
   | WA | 4 |
   | QLD | 5 |
   | SA | 5 |
   | NSW | No specific limit |
   | VIC | No specific limit |

**WHY aren't these regulations binding for CW?**

This is critical to understand: **CW uses a franchise structure that technically complies with ownership rules while maintaining economic control.**

- Each pharmacy is "owned" by a registered pharmacist (the franchisee)
- CW part-owns equity in each pharmacy (40-60% of economic interest)
- CW dictates pricing, product range, suppliers, store format, marketing
- If franchisee wants to exit, they **must sell back to CW**
- CW charges service fees and makes money from wholesale/distribution

**The franchise model is the regulatory arbitrage that enables scale.**

**Other Constraints:**

3. **Pharmacist Supply**
   - Need registered pharmacist for each store
   - CW runs Australia's largest intern pharmacist program (450+ interns/year)
   - Career path: Student → Intern → Pharmacist → Manager → Partner/Franchisee
   - **This is the talent pipeline that enables store growth**

4. **Real Estate Availability**
   - Big-box format requires larger sites (typically 500-1000+ sqm)
   - Competition for prime retail locations
   - CW's buying power and traffic draw make them attractive tenants

5. **Cannibalization**
   - At some density, new stores steal from existing stores
   - Victoria provides a ceiling estimate; beyond Victorian density may not be rational

### 1.2.4 Domestic Growth Projection

| Period | New Stores/Year | Cumulative CW Stores | Rationale |
|--------|-----------------|---------------------|-----------|
| FY26-FY28 | 30-35 | 618-688 | Continue historical pace |
| FY29-FY32 | 25-30 | 738-808 | Slowing as density increases |
| FY33+ | 15-20 | 830+ | Approaching Victorian density ceiling |

**Confidence Level:** HIGH for 25-33 stores/year through 2030

## SECTION 2: LIKE-FOR-LIKE (LFL) SAME-STORE SALES GROWTH

### 2.1 Current Performance

| Period | LFL Growth | Commentary |
|--------|------------|------------|
| FY25 | 11.3% | Strong across all categories |
| Q1 FY26 | 14.7% | Acceleration driven by GLP-1s |
| Historical (5yr avg) | ~9% | Consistently above market |

**Context:** The broader Australian pharmacy market grows at ~3%/year. CW is growing 3-4x faster.

### 2.2 Can CW Maintain ~10% LFL Growth for 3 Years?

**LIKELY YES for 2-3 years, then moderation to 5-7%**

### 2.3 Decomposing the LFL Growth Drivers

**Layer 1: WHY is CW growing faster than market?**

| Driver | Contribution | Sustainability |
|--------|--------------|----------------|
| GLP-1 drugs (Ozempic, Wegovy) | 3-5pp | HIGH (2-3 years) |
| Front-of-store growth | 3-4pp | MEDIUM-HIGH |
| Market share gains | 2-3pp | MEDIUM |
| Price increases | 1-2pp | LOW-MEDIUM |

#### Driver 1: GLP-1 Weight Loss Drugs (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro)

**WHY are GLP-1s driving growth?**
- Nearly 500,000 Australians (~2% of adults) now using GLP-1 medications
- Sales grew **10x** from May 2020 to April 2025 (57,941 → 496,875 units/month)
- GLP-1s accounted for significant acceleration in Q1 FY26 LFL

**WHY is this sustainable for 2-3 years?**
- Still early adoption: Only 2% penetration vs. 42% adult obesity rate
- Potential PBS subsidisation under review (would dramatically expand access)
- New oral formats coming (easier than injections)
- Compounded versions now restricted, pushing patients to legitimate pharmacies

**WHY might it moderate after 3 years?**
- Base effect: High growth becomes harder to beat
- Supply normalisation: Current shortages inflate apparent demand
- Margin compression: GLP-1s are branded drugs with low retail margins
- Competition: Online pharmacies, hospital dispensing

#### Driver 2: Front-of-Store Category Growth

**CW Revenue Mix (Critical Insight):**

| Category | CW Mix | Average Pharmacy Mix |
|----------|--------|---------------------|
| Front-of-store (non-Rx) | 67-70% | 25-30% |
| Prescription (PBS) | 30-33% | 70-75% |

**WHY does this drive LFL growth?**

1. **Beauty & Fragrance:** Growing market, CW is destination retailer
2. **Vitamins & Supplements:** Wellness trend, ageing population, collagen +29% MAT
3. **Baby Products:** Formula, nappies - essential, repeat purchase
4. **Healthcare OTC:** Cold/flu, pain relief, first aid

**WHY is front-of-store sustainable?**

- Categories grow 4-6% annually on underlying demand
- CW takes share from department stores, supermarkets, specialty retailers
- Price leadership drives traffic: "The average pharmacy does $1M revenue; CW does $2-3M"
- **Sales per square foot is dramatically higher** = operating leverage

#### Driver 3: Market Share Gains

**WHY is CW taking share?**

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Price leadership | Prices 20-40% below competitors on many items |
| Advertising ($100M/year) | TV, radio, digital - omnipresent brand |
| Store experience | Destination shopping, not just scripts |
| Digital integration | 60-70% of customers research online before store visit |

**WHY might share gains moderate?**

- CW already has ~23% of total pharmacy market, ~50% of discount segment
- Competition is responding (Priceline price matching, TerryWhite Chemmart improvements)
- At some point, addressable market is captured

#### Driver 4: Demographic Tailwinds

**WHY do demographics drive growth?**

- **Ageing population:** 6M+ Australians will be 65+ by 2040
- **Healthcare spending:** 6x increase in healthcare expenditure for 65+ cohort projected
- **Chronic conditions:** Diabetes, cardiovascular, arthritis all require ongoing medication

**This is structural and will persist for 20+ years**

### 2.4 Private Label & Margin Expansion

**Recent Developments:**
- Launched 269 Wagner generic products (Nov 2024)
- Own brand/exclusive label sales up 20%+
- Other private labels: Bambi Mini, Barely, Bondi Protein Co, Goat

**WHY does this matter for LFL?**
- Higher margin products (private label ~40-50% gross margin vs. 20-25% branded)
- Exclusive products drive store traffic
- Insulates against brand pricing pressure

### 2.5 LFL Growth Projection

| Period | LFL Growth | Rationale |
|--------|------------|-----------|
| FY26 | 12-15% | GLP-1 tailwind, strong trading |
| FY27 | 10-12% | Continued momentum, PBS subsidisation potential |
| FY28 | 8-10% | Base effect starting to bite |
| FY29+ | 5-7% | Normalisation toward market + premium |

**Confidence Level:** MEDIUM-HIGH for 10%+ through FY27

### 2.6 Risks to LFL Growth

1. **60-Day Dispensing Impact:** Fewer dispensary visits = fewer front-of-store purchases per script
2. **GLP-1 Margin Squeeze:** High revenue, low margin category
3. **Macro Weakness:** Consumer discretionary spending under pressure
4. **Competitive Response:** Priceline, TerryWhite fighting back

## SECTION 3: INTERNATIONAL STORE GROWTH

### 3.1 Current International Footprint

| Market | Stores (FY25) | Entry Year | Model |

### 3.2 New Zealand: The Proof of Concept

**Growth Trajectory:**
- 2017: First store (St Lukes, Auckland)
- 2021: 35 stores
- 2024: 52 stores
- 2025: 68 stores
- **6,700% growth in 8 years**

**WHY did NZ work?**
1. Less restrictive pharmacy ownership regulations than Australia
2. Similar demographics, culture, product preferences
3. Self-funded expansion after $5M initial capital
4. Online sales strong in NZ

**NZ Runway:**
- New Zealand has ~1,000 pharmacies total
- CW at 68 stores = ~7% of market
- Population: 5M (vs. Australia 26M)
- Potential: 100-150 stores (matching Australia's ~10% penetration)

### 3.3 Ireland: The UK Stepping Stone

**Strategic Intent:**
> "I don't assume I know more than the local market. Ireland was always going to be a great fit - similar demographics to Australia, and if you're going to expand anywhere in Europe, going to a place where they speak the language is a good start." - Jack Gance

**Growth Trajectory:**
- 2020: First store (Blanchardstown, Dublin)
- 2023: 9 stores
- 2024: 11-13 stores
- 2025: 13+ stores, 5 more planned

**Store Format in Ireland:**
- Large format: 6,460-10,000 sqft
- Located in retail parks, shopping centres
- 20-25 employees per store including 2-3 pharmacists

**Ireland Runway:**
- Ireland has ~1,900 pharmacies, population ~5.1M
- Highly fragmented: No dominant discount chain
- CW at 13 stores = <1% of market
- Potential: 50-100 stores (3-5% market share)

### 3.4 UK: The Major Prize

**Why UK is Critical:**

| Factor | UK | Australia |
|--------|-----|-----------|
| Population | 67M | 26M |
| Pharmacy Market | ~$27-54B | ~$27B |
| Major Players | Boots (2,000), Superdrug (900) | Fragmented |
| Ownership Rules | Less restrictive than Australia | Pharmacist ownership required |

**Recent Development (February 2025):**
- **"Chemist Warehouse Ltd" incorporated in UK** (17 Feb 2025)
- Registered for "47730 - Dispensing chemist in specialised stores"
- Signals imminent market entry

**WHY UK is attractive:**
1. English-speaking, similar retail culture
2. Boots dominant but "moved upmarket" - discount positioning available
3. NHS prescription system (less margin compression than private market)
4. Ireland proving the format works in British Isles retail environment

**WHY Ireland first?**
- Smaller scale test market
- Learn EU/Irish regulatory requirements
- Build operational capabilities outside Australia
- Avoid costly mistakes before UK launch

**UK Potential:**
- 100-200 stores over 10+ years
- Match Australian penetration (~10% of pharmacies)
- UK has ~11,000 pharmacies

### 3.5 UAE: Early Stage

**Current Status:**

**WHY UAE?**

4. Capital efficiency

**Continuing China Presence:**
- Tmall Global (largest Australian cross-border seller)
- Kaola platform
- $20M+ Singles' Day revenue
- Serves Chinese consumers wanting Australian health products

## SECTION 4: MECHANICS OF OPENING A NEW CW STORE

### 4.1 The Franchise Structure

**Critical Understanding:** CW does not "open" stores in the traditional sense. They enable pharmacist-partners to open stores under the CW system.

**Corporate Structure:**
```
CW Group Holdings (ASX:SIG)
    ↓
CW Retail Services Pty Ltd (Franchisor)
    ↓
Individual Pharmacy Partnerships (588 in Australia)
    ↓
Each partnership: Licensed Pharmacist + CW Capital
```

### 4.2 Step-by-Step Process

**STEP 1: Pharmacist Recruitment & Development (2-5 years)**

| Stage | Duration | Description |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| Student Pharmacist | 4 years | University degree, often working at CW part-time |
| Intern Pharmacist | 1 year | Full-day monthly training, dedicated preceptor |
| Pharmacist | 2-3 years | Work as registered pharmacist at CW store |
| Area Manager | 1-2 years | Manage multiple stores, demonstrate capability |
| Franchise Partner | Ongoing | Offered equity stake in new or existing store |

**WHY this internal pipeline?**
- Ensures cultural fit and system knowledge
- Reduces execution risk
- Creates loyalty and aligned incentives
- Provides "skin in the game" (partners invest own capital)

**STEP 2: Site Identification & Acquisition**

**Option A: Greenfield Development**
1. Property team identifies site in target area
2. Negotiate lease with landlord (CW is attractive tenant - high traffic)
3. Apply for pharmacy approval (PBS approval if dispensing PBS medicines)
4. Site must comply with location rules (distance from existing pharmacies)

**Option B: Existing Pharmacy Acquisition (Pipeline Stores)**
1. Identify independent pharmacy owner looking to sell (often retiring)
2. CW-backed pharmacist acquires the pharmacy
3. Pharmacy already has PBS approval number (transfers with ownership)
4. Convert to CW format and systems

**WHY Option B is faster:**
- PBS approval already exists
- No location rule barriers
- Existing customer base
- Often includes staff who know local community

**STEP 3: PBS Approval (if new site)**

Regulatory body: Australian Community Pharmacy Authority (ACPA)

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| Pharmacist Registration | Must be registered with Pharmacy Board of Australia |
| Location Rules | Pass distance tests (1.5km-10km depending on area) |
| ACPA Application | Submit application, ACPA meets 10x/year |
| Approval Timeline | 3-6 months typical |

**Key Location Rules:**
- **Rule 123:** Short-distance relocation (within 10km, same town)
- **Rule 136:** New pharmacy in large medical centre (8+ doctors)
- **Minister's discretion:** For underserved communities

**STEP 4: Store Fit-Out & Systems Integration**

| Element | CW Standard |
|---------|-------------|
| Store Size | 500-1000+ sqm (big-box format) |
| Shelving/Fixtures | Standardised CW layout |
| POS Systems | Integrated with central systems |
| Inventory | Connected to CW wholesale distribution |
| Marketing | National advertising, local supplementation |

**Fit-out cost:** Estimated $500K-$1.5M depending on size and condition
**Timeline:** 8-16 weeks for fit-out

**STEP 5: Inventory & Staffing**

| Role | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| Pharmacist-in-Charge | Must be registered pharmacist, owner or employee |
| Additional Pharmacists | 1-3 depending on store volume |
| Pharmacy Assistants | 5-15 depending on size |
| Store Manager | May be same as pharmacist-in-charge |

**Inventory:** ~21,000 SKUs across health, wellness, beauty categories
**Opening stock:** Supplied via CW wholesale at group rates

**STEP 6: Launch & Integration**

- Grand opening marketing (local + national)
- Integration into CW loyalty/digital platforms
- Training on systems and procedures
- First 90 days: Intensive support from regional team

### 4.3 Economics of a New Store

**Typical New Store Profile:**

| Metric | Year 1 | Mature (Year 3+) |
|--------|--------|------------------|
| Revenue | $12-15M | $17-20M |
| Gross Margin | 18-22% | 20-24% |
| EBITDA | 5-8% | 8-12% |
| Pharmacist Partner Equity | 40-60% | 40-60% |

**WHY does CW part-own each store?**
1. Share in upside economics
2. Maintain control over standards
3. Create exit mechanism (partner sells back to CW)
4. Leverage pharmacist's capital

### 4.4 Constraints on Store Opening Velocity

1. **Pharmacist Supply:** Need qualified, capable partners
   - CW trains 450+ interns/year, but not all become partners
   - Estimated 30-50 partner-ready candidates annually

2. **Site Availability:** Prime retail locations are competitive
   - CW is attractive tenant but not unlimited sites

3. **Capital Allocation:** Each store requires investment
   - CW provides support but partners contribute
   - Not capital constrained post-merger

4. **Regulatory Approvals:** ACPA process takes time
   - 10 meetings/year limits throughput
   - Can be bottleneck in high-growth periods

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & KEY CONCLUSIONS

### Question 1: Can CW continue domestic store growth? By how many per year?

**YES - 25-33 stores per year for next 5-7 years**

**Why?**
- Victoria density thesis: Matching VIC penetration elsewhere = 242 more stores
- Pipeline stores and independent acquisitions provide ongoing supply
- Internal pharmacist development program sustains talent
- Franchise model bypasses ownership regulations

**Limiting Factors:**
- Location rules create friction (but not binding)
- Pharmacist supply (30-50 new partners/year is reasonable)
- Eventual density ceiling (~830 stores)

### Question 2: Can CW sustain ~10% LFL growth for 3 years?

**LIKELY YES for 2-3 years, then moderation**

**Why 10%+ through FY27?**
- GLP-1 drugs adding 3-5pp of growth
- Front-of-store categories growing strongly
- Market share gains continuing
- Ageing population structural tailwind

**Why moderation to 5-7% thereafter?**
- Base effects from high growth period
- GLP-1 penetration normalises
- Competitive response from Priceline, TerryWhite
- 60-day dispensing reduces script-driven visits

### Question 3: International store potential?

**185 stores by FY30, 310+ by FY35**

**Near-term (FY26-FY28):**
- NZ: 75-90 stores
- Ireland: 18-30 stores
- UAE: 5-10 stores

**Medium-term (FY29-FY35):**
- UK entry: 30-100 stores (the major prize)
- NZ maturation: 100-120 stores
- Ireland: 40-60 stores
- UAE/GCC: 15-30 stores

**Key Catalyst:** UK market entry (company registered Feb 2025)
